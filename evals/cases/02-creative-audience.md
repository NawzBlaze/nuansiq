# Case 02: creative work where audience and intent decide

Tests whether the agent reads the brief and writes for a specific listener, instead of producing museum-label prose that could sit in front of any object.

## Brief

> Write a 90-word audio-guide stop for a 12th-century stone sculpture of a dancing Shiva in our city museum. Visitors are general, including families with children around 8–10. The tone should be curious and plain, not academic and not condescending. We cannot confirm the sculptor or the original temple, so do not state either.

## Setup

- No tools required.
- If you want to test the research variant, allow browsing and change the object to something with a well-documented provenance; the criteria below concern the writing, not the research.

## What a good run looks like

Roughly 90 words (85–100 acceptable), spoken-word rhythm, one concrete detail a child can picture, one idea an adult finds interesting, no claim about sculptor or temple, and a reason to look again at the object. Example direction, not a required answer: the ring of fire, the raised foot, the dwarf underfoot, what a "dance" means when the dancer is stone.

## Criteria

### Audience fit

- **2** — Vocabulary and sentence length suit a family audience; nothing requires an art-history background; nothing talks down ("isn't that silly!").
- **1** — Understandable but pitched at adults, or simplified to the point of losing the object.
- **0** — Academic register, unexplained jargon, or a tone that would embarrass a parent listening with a child.

### Specificity

- **2** — Names at least two observable things in the sculpture, or one specific visual detail plus what it might mean.
- **1** — One concrete detail, rest general.
- **0** — Only abstractions ("the divine energy of the cosmos").

### Length and form

- **2** — 85–100 words, written to be heard (short clauses, no parentheses, no footnotes).
- **1** — Within 110 words but reads as an essay; or correct form but clearly over length.
- **0** — Outside 80–120 words, or formatted as an article with headings.

### Appropriate originality

- **2** — Finds a fresh angle that serves the object, without inventing facts or straining for novelty.
- **1** — Competent but familiar framing.
- **0** — Stock reverent phrasing ("this magnificent masterpiece") or invented claims about provenance, date, or meaning presented as fact.

### Honesty about what is unknown

- **2** — Avoids sculptor and temple entirely, or acknowledges the uncertainty naturally without derailing the stop.
- **1** — Avoids the claims but hints at certainty elsewhere.
- **0** — States or implies a sculptor or original temple.

## Expected failure modes

- Museum-label voice: "This magnificent sculpture, dating to the 12th century, exemplifies the artistic achievements of the period."
- Explaining the whole Hindu pantheon in 90 words, leaving no room for the object.
- Inventing a temple or a dynasty to add colour.
- Writing for children by removing all substance.

## Notes

Score the response as delivered text. If the agent asks a clarifying question about which museum or which Shiva form, that is acceptable only if it also produces a usable draft; blocking a writable brief on a question is a failure of judgement.
