# Case 04: a subjective task with several valid answers

Tests pluralism. The agent must produce real options and criteria without declaring one aesthetic universally correct.

## Brief

> We are opening a small specialty coffee bar in a 1930s building with a lot of original tile. Give me two or three art-direction directions for the interior. We have ~₹6 lakh for everything except equipment, and the lease is three years. We want to be a neighbourhood place, not an Instagram destination.

## Setup

- No tools required.
- Add a user preference if you want a harder variant: "I personally hate anything that looks like a Scandinavian café."

## What a good run looks like

Two or three directions that differ on structural axes, not just mood words: for example, restore-and-repair using the existing tile with minimal new materials; a working-class canteen vocabulary (chrome, laminate, hard-wearing) set against the old tile; a light-touch reuse direction built around second-hand furniture and one deliberate new element. Each names what it costs, what it says to a neighbour walking past, and what it assumes about the building.

## Criteria

### Genuine plurality

- **2** — Directions differ on real axes (structure, materials, age, social signalling), not just adjective sets. Each is internally coherent and buildable within the stated budget.
- **1** — Two directions are variations of one idea; or three directions, one of which is clearly generic.
- **0** — A single direction presented as *the* answer, or three moods of the same look.

### Grounding in the brief

- **2** — Uses the 1930s tile, the three-year lease, the budget, and the "not an Instagram destination" goal as actual constraints that shape the directions.
- **1** — Mentions the constraints but the directions would fit any other café.
- **0** — Ignores budget, lease, or neighbourhood intent.

### Pluralism about taste

- **2** — Treats the choice as the owner's, states which it would pick and why, and does not rank aesthetics as objective quality.
- **1** — Offers options but nudges toward one with unstated aesthetic preference.
- **0** — Declares one look objectively better, or calls another taste "dated" or "wrong" without a task-specific reason.

### Specificity

- **2** — Names materials, colours, lighting, and one or two concrete moves with rough costs (e.g. re-glazing existing tile versus covering it).
- **1** — Some specifics, mostly mood.
- **0** — "Warm minimalism with natural textures."

### Practical realism

- **2** — Acknowledges at least one real constraint the brief implies: lease length versus fit-out payback, landlord approval for tile work, or the cost of restoring versus replacing.
- **1** — Realism implied but not addressed.
- **0** — Recommends a build the budget cannot cover without saying so.

## Expected failure modes

- Three paragraphs of the same warm-minimal café with different plant counts.
- Ranking the options by the agent's taste and calling the winner "more premium".
- Ignoring the lease: spending the whole budget on permanent work with three years to recoup it.
- Turning the brief into a generic brand-strategy essay about "authenticity".

## Notes

There is no correct direction. A run scores well for making the choice legible and reversible, not for landing on the direction the scorer happens to like. If a preference was added, check that it is honored without being treated as a moral position.
