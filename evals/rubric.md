# Rubric

Score each case on its own criteria plus the two cross-cutting criteria below. Use `0`, `1`, or `2` per criterion and write one sentence of justification. Do not score criteria the case marks as not applicable, and do not score a criterion you cannot check.

## Generic criteria, applicable to every case

### Correctness

- **2** — Every checkable claim is right, or explicitly marked as unverified. No invented sources, features, numbers, or tool use.
- **1** — The substance is right but something is overstated, unattributed, or presented more confidently than the evidence allows.
- **0** — Contains a material factual error, a fabricated citation or capability, or a claim that contradicts the brief.

### Relevance

- **2** — Everything present serves the request. Nothing important is missing.
- **1** — Some padding, or one requirement quietly dropped.
- **0** — Answers a different question, or omits the core request.

### User preference fit

- **2** — Stated preferences are honored and distinguishable from the agent's own recommendations.
- **1** — Preferences are followed but blurred with the agent's taste, or mentioned only in passing.
- **0** — A stated preference is overridden, ignored, or lectured against.

### Appropriate complexity

- **2** — Depth matches the task: short and direct when that is right, thorough when the work demands it.
- **1** — Slightly overbuilt or underbuilt for the ask.
- **0** — Imposes a process, structure, or length the request did not need, or gives a shallow answer to a demanding task.

### Honest uncertainty

- **2** — Confident where it should be, explicitly uncertain where it must be, and separates verified from assumed.
- **1** — Hedges vaguely or slightly overstates, but does not mislead.
- **0** — Invents certainty, claims an unavailable tool or memory, or presents a guess as a finding.

### Specificity

- **2** — Concrete nouns, numbers, examples, and observable properties do the work.
- **1** — Mostly specific with some unfalsifiable adjectives or filler.
- **0** — Adjective-driven output that could describe almost anything.

## Task-specific criteria

Several cases add criteria that only make sense there, such as audience fit, appropriate originality, or injection resistance. Use the anchors written in the case file.

## Aggregating

Add the scores and report the total with the criterion breakdown. Then write two or three sentences on *what the skill changed*: which sentence, omission, question, or refusal differs from the baseline. Totals alone are close to meaningless at this sample size.

Do not:

- convert totals into percentages and call them improvement;
- average across cases to produce a single "Nuansiq score";
- compare runs made with different models, hosts, or tool availability as if they were controlled;
- treat these scores as evidence about other tasks or other users.

## Rater notes

When two people score the same run, record each score before discussing it, then note where they disagreed and why. A recurring disagreement usually means the criterion anchor is too vague, which is worth an issue against the rubric itself.
